# Decisions and open questions

## Decided
- One all-in-one package. Setup fee SAR 14,999 (40% at signing, 30% at configuration, 30% after go-live). Subscription SAR 1,200/year (software only). Managed service SAR 2,500/month.
- Plans: Pilot (discounted setup for first 3-5 clients, in return for feedback) and Standard (full price). Managed service is a separate option on either.
- Teal and white palette, left sidebar, top bar with search and EN | AR, light and dark mode.
- Admin is a separate console. Clients page = total, search, add client. Add client = separate page with full registration details.
- Build order: foundation, sales/purchases/bank, reports and review, ZATCA then UAE, portal/calendar, HRM/CRM/restaurant, pilot.

- Stock is valued at weighted average cost (PRODUCT_SPEC). Goods move in with a goods received note (GRN) and out with a goods delivery note (GDN); a GRN credits 2050 Goods received not yet billed, which the supplier's bill clears.
- Depreciation is monthly straight line, counting the month the asset starts as a full month. Missed months are caught up in the next run.
- Bank statements are imported as CSV or Excel files. A file is matched to the client's bank account by the IBAN or account number in it; the column layout is detected or set once per account.

- Each client has an industry, chosen at registration. It sets the optional modules (Inventory, Restaurant, Assets, Projects, HRM, CRM); a restaurant gets Restaurant instead of Inventory. The Administrator can change them.
- Restaurant: materials (stock items), materials in (goods received), menu items with recipes (materials per portion), waste, and food sales. Each food sale issues a tax invoice, takes the recipe materials out of stock at average cost and records the payment, all at once. A sale that would take a material below zero is stopped; materials are received first.

- PDFs are A4 pages drawn by the browser and saved with its print window ("Save as PDF"), so Arabic is always shaped correctly. Sales documents are bilingual (English and Arabic). In Saudi Arabia an invoice is a "Tax invoice" when the buyer has a VAT number and a "Simplified tax invoice" otherwise, with the ZATCA phase 1 QR code (seller, VAT number, time, total, VAT). Phase 2 (XML, cryptographic stamp, clearance through Fatoora) comes with the ZATCA adapter.
- Reports export to PDF and to Excel (.xlsx), numbers as numbers.
- HRM is full payroll for Saudi Arabia and the UAE: monthly payroll with GOSI (Saudi Arabia) and GPSSA (UAE nationals), overtime, unpaid leave, absence and sick pay tiers by the labour laws, annual leave balances, loans and advances, a monthly end of service provision and final settlements, payslips, salary files (UAE WPS SIF; Saudi payroll file for Mudad or the bank) and HR documents with expiry dates. A month's pay is worked on a 30-day month. Oman (PASI) comes with Oman.
- To confirm before the first real payroll: GOSI and GPSSA rates and caps (src/books/payroll.ts), the WPS SIF layout with the client's bank or exchange house, and the Mudad payroll file layout.
- CRM is light and never posts to the books: leads, a deal pipeline by stage (chance 10/25/50/75%), activities and campaigns. A deal can create a Sales quotation; won and lost follow that quotation (invoiced = won, declined = lost). Sending messages from campaigns (email, SMS, WhatsApp) is not built; it needs a provider.
- Review layer: firm staff on a client (partner, reviewer, staff accountant) run the checks, raise queries, tick the checklist and explain findings; only partners and reviewers sign off. Everyone on the client sees Review and can answer queries (Review is not part of client roles). Sign-off needs the month ended, the checklist done and no open queries; it does not lock the period (locking stays a partner's separate step).
- Selling to companies directly: Nusuh Books (the platform owner) creates every customer, its first Administrator and every company, for accounting firms too. A firm's Administrator manages staff, client users, roles and access and can still edit its clients' details; a company's Administrator manages users, roles, access and activity only and sees only its own company. A company reviews its own books through client roles with review and sign-off rights. The owner can suspend a customer (nobody there can sign in; books kept).
- In reports, projects are kept apart from cost centres: P&L by cost centre shows branches and departments, with projects as one line pointing to the Projects report.

- Reports show account names, not account codes. The profit and loss reads: Sales, less Cost of sales = Gross profit, less Indirect expenses, add Indirect income = Net profit. Each income and expense account has its line (set automatically, changeable in Ledger > Chart of accounts).
- Client roles: the Administrator defines job titles for client users in Roles and permissions, each with None, View or View and enter per module, and whether it may approve expense claims. Every role is editable: Client accountant (everything) and Client management (view everything) are created for each firm as ordinary roles, and every client user has a role. Firm staff keep their fixed roles. A role in use cannot be archived.
- Reports added: owner summary with ratios (gross and net margin, current and quick ratio, debtor and creditor days, working capital), balance sheet (non-current and current assets and liabilities; this year's profit shown in equity until the year is closed), cash flow by the direct method (bank movements grouped as operating, investing and financing by the other side of each entry), P&L comparisons and monthly columns, and the VAT return worksheet laid out by the return boxes from the books. The worksheet is for checking before filing; box layout to be confirmed with ZATCA and the FTA. Every account in a report opens its ledger.
- Client portal: requests, documents both ways and monthly reports between the firm and its client, inside the same login (no separate portal site). Portal and Calendar are open to everyone on the client, like Review; client roles do not switch them off. A view-only client user can still send documents. Not shown to a company that keeps its own books. Nothing in the portal posts to the books; sending email notices waits for an email provider.
- Compliance calendar: deadlines are worked out from the company's country, VAT status and registration date, financial year end, CR expiry, modules and calendar settings, never typed in; only "done" (with the filing reference) and the client's own deadlines are stored. Rules used: Saudi VAT by the end of the month after the period, zakat 120 days after the year end, GOSI by the 15th; UAE VAT by the 28th day of the month after the period, corporate tax 9 months after the year end, GPSSA by the 10th, WPS salaries by the 15th; Oman VAT within 30 days.
- E-invoicing (step 4) is one adapter per country on the same invoice data (src/einvoice). Saudi Arabia: ZATCA phase 2 built to the published XML Implementation and Security Features standards as the Fatoora SDK applies them (UBL 2.1, invoice hash over C14N 1.1 without the signature and QR, ECDSA secp256k1, XAdES signed properties, QR tags 1-9, ICV and PIH chain, API version 2). One device (EGS unit) per company; its key is encrypted with ENCRYPTION_KEY; the chain is enforced in the database. Every invoice and credit note is e-invoiced from the moment a device is connected; a tax invoice to a Saudi business is refused without the buyer's national address. UAE: Peppol PINT AE XML for B2B and B2G (buyer with a TRN), handed to the accredited service provider; B2C is outside UAE e-invoicing for now.
- Debit notes are not part of the books yet; the ZATCA compliance checks use debit-note samples only.
- Approvals (step 5) cover supplier bills, supplier payments and customer credit notes, each from an amount set per client by an approver; expenses are recorded after the money is spent and stay outside (employee claims keep their own approval). Single level: one approver, never the sender. What would stop issuing (missing cost centre, locked period) is checked when it is sent, so nothing is approved that cannot be issued.
- The cash flow forecast is worked out from the books each time (nothing stored but people's own items and the minimum cash). Payroll repeats last month's net pay; social insurance repeats last month's contributions on the calendar's due date; VAT due is the VAT payable of each return period so far.
- Bulk import goes through the same services as typing (rules, audit, separation). Customers, suppliers, items and accounts: new rows are added, existing ones (same name or VAT number, code, account code) are left as they are, rows with problems are left out with the reason. Opening balances: all or nothing, one entry. Files are read in the browser; only the rows are sent.
- What is still to build is kept in docs/PENDING.md, by priority.

## Open
- Restaurant POS integration (importing sales from the restaurant's own POS instead of entering them in Nusuh Books): which POS systems.
- Direct bank feeds (transactions sent by the bank, no files): needs an open banking provider licensed by SAMA (Saudi) or under the UAE Central Bank open finance framework. Which provider, and the cost.
- Selling a fixed asset with VAT: today the disposal records proceeds before VAT; how the VAT on the sale is invoiced is to be agreed with the reviewer.
- Which country signs first (Saudi or UAE): sets the e-invoicing order.
- What the SAR 2,500 managed fee includes; pilot discount amount.
- How clients use cost centres (branch, department, project).
- Which POS systems restaurant clients use.
- Developer to hire; hosting region (check Saudi and UAE data protection rules, take legal advice).
- Before the first real client on ZATCA: run the onboarding against the ZATCA sandbox and simulation from the server (this build was tested offline: the sandbox was not reachable from the build environment), and confirm with ZATCA: VAT per category is the sum of the line VAT (the books' rounding) rather than recalculated on the total; one exemption reason per company for zero-rated and exempt lines (ZATCA may need it per line); the Fatoora SDK's validator on our XML.
- UAE: choose the accredited service provider (Phase 1 appointment deadline was 30 October 2026; go-live 1 January 2027 for AED 50 million and above, 1 July 2027 for the rest), then check our PINT AE XML with its validator and connect its API.
- Oman Fawtara: dates and specs with the Oman Tax Authority.
- Confirm the calendar's deadline rules with ZATCA, the FTA, GOSI, GPSSA and MOHRE (src/books/compliance.ts), and whether Saudi Mudad/WPS salary deadlines should be added.
- Email or SMS reminders for deadlines, portal requests and approvals: needs a sending provider.
- Approvals: whether some clients need two levels (for example a second approver above a higher amount).
